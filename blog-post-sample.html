Regulated markets are where carbon strategy gets real

Equiterra Team · 21 August 2026 · 5 min read
← Back to Blog

Beyond the voluntary market, carbon credits settle real obligations: domestic carbon taxes across Latin America, airline offsetting under CORSIA, and country-to-country transfers under Article 6. We structure supply, procurement, and transactions inside these systems.

Where we operate

  • Colombia — carbon tax offset mechanism (no causación): eligible-credit sourcing, structuring and delivery for obligated fuel consumers.
  • Chile — green tax offset system: forward structures that monetize reductions against emissions-tax liabilities.
  • Mexico — registry-based trades serving state-level compliance and low-emissions certification schemes.
  • CORSIA — sourcing and structuring of CORSIA-eligible units for airlines and aircraft operators, aligned with ICAO eligibility and corresponding-adjustment requirements.
  • Government-to-government (Article 6.2) — bilateral cooperation structures: authorization strategy, corresponding adjustments, and ITMO transfer frameworks between sovereigns.

Why it matters

Most carbon market commentary stays focused on the voluntary market. But for obligated buyers — airlines under CORSIA, fuel importers under a domestic carbon tax, governments transacting under Article 6 — the rules, eligibility screens and settlement mechanics are different, and getting them wrong is expensive. We work inside these systems from mandate to retirement: eligibility screening, counterparty diligence, pricing and settlement.
